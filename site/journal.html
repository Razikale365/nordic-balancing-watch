Edge cases and API quirks in the sources’ data

Nordic Balancing Watch is in development; collection started on 24 October 2026. These notes record, by date, the edge cases, API quirks and problems met in the sources’ data, and how the page handles each. Collected values are never edited or deleted. A value found to come from a collection fault is marked with the reason and left out of the figures.

11 November 2026: a value filled in late looks like a corrected one

What was found

Fingrid showed the Finnish mFRR capacity prices for the delivery day of 17 October 2026 as 0 EUR/MW in every hour. On 30 October it showed the procured prices and volumes instead. ENTSO-E had those same prices and volumes for that day from the first check on 24 October, for all 24 hours in both directions. So the zeros were values not yet filled in, not an auction result that was changed afterwards.

In the collected data the two cases look the same: a first value, then a different one. No source says why it changes a price.

How the page handles it

A source’s change to a published price is called a revision. The page says when it happened, not why. A capacity price changed after delivery is described as a change that a later auction does not explain.

11 November 2026: Svenska kraftnät’s capacity prices appear after the delivery day has started

What was found

Hourly checks that look 48 hours ahead returned no rows for a Swedish delivery day until about 02:00 UTC on that day, when the whole day arrived at once: 192 hourly prices on 6 November (24 hours, two directions, four zones). The delivery day starts at 23:00 UTC, so its first three hours are first seen after they have started. In the week to 8 November, the prices of 221 of 1,325 mFRR delivery hours (17%) and 55 of 279 aFRR delivery hours (20%) were first seen after delivery had started.

How the page handles it

Lead is measured for each delivery quarter-hour and can be negative. The reserve table shows it, and the page says where a product is not always seen ahead of delivery.

11 November 2026: a price can arrive days late at every source

What was found

Three quarter-hours in the first two weeks were late at every source at once:

  • The one starting 30 October, 14:30 UTC was still missing 48 hours after it ended and was first seen at most 85 hours after, in all 12 zones at eSett and ENTSO-E, at Energinet and at Fingrid.
  • The one starting 3 November, 15:45 UTC first appeared 17 to 22 hours after it ended at Energinet, ENTSO-E, Fingrid and eSett, and after 41 hours for Denmark at eSett.
  • The one starting 6 November, 15:30 UTC took 44 to 45 hours at Energinet, ENTSO-E, Fingrid and eSett’s Norwegian and Swedish zones. eSett’s prices for Denmark and Finland were still missing 48 hours after it ended and were first seen at most 84 hours after.

Every source was late together, so the delay began before publication rather than at one publisher.

How the page handles it

The 15-minute checks look back 48 hours. A price later than that is found by a once-a-day check, so its publication time is not known to within a day. It is counted as late and left out of the timings. “Slowest timed” is therefore the slowest price that was timed, not always the slowest there was.

11 November 2026: Energinet’s Danish mFRR capacity prices for two days appeared days late

What was found

Energinet’s mFRR capacity prices for DK1 and DK2 are normally first seen at about 07:45 UTC on the day before delivery. For the delivery days of 29 and 30 October 2026, every hourly check up to and through delivery found no price, in either direction. The prices were first seen on 3 November at 03:30 UTC, up to 124 hours after the delivery hours they belong to had ended: 48 hourly prices per zone and direction.

How the page handles it

A capacity price first seen more than 48 hours after delivery is counted as late and left out of the lead times, so a late day does not pass as a short lead.

11 November 2026: a revision can come two hours or ten days after the price

What was found

In the first 16 days, sources changed 22 published imbalance prices. A Finnish price of 25 October changed about two hours after it appeared, and one of 2 November within a day. Seventeen Norwegian prices for 25 October, 22:00 to 22:45 UTC (NO1 to NO5) changed three days later, at eSett and ENTSO-E together. Two Finnish prices of 29 October changed after six days. Later, prices of 1 November for Finland and SE1 changed after ten. Of the 9,509 first prices seen from 24 to 27 October, counting each source, 37 (0.4%) had been changed two weeks later.

How the page handles it

A price first seen this week can still change, so the share of first prices changed later follows prices that have been watched for 14 days or more. The log lists every change as it is seen.

11 November 2026: eSett’s Finnish 0.00 stand-in follows the clock

What was found

From 25 October to 7 November, eSett’s 0.00 stand-in for Finland was only ever seen for quarter-hours starting on the hour (325 of 336) or at a quarter past (237 of 336), never for the other two (0 of 671). It appears about an hour after the interval ends and is replaced by the settled price about two hours later. The other quarter-hours get the settled price directly, 75 to 105 minutes after they end.

How the page handles it

As before: a 0.00 that eSett later replaces, and that Fingrid never showed, counts neither as a first price nor as a revision.

9 November 2026: Energinet’s FCR price is a running total until the last auction

What was found

Energinet publishes its FCR prices (FCR-N and FCR-D up and down, in DK2 and SE1–SE4, one joint market) as a “Total”: a volume-weighted average of the auctions cleared so far. When the later D-1 auction clears, about 18 hours after first publication, the Total changes. Every such change with evidence in the collected log was an auction being added. In the week to 8 November a later auction completed 453 hourly FCR prices of the joint market, against 9 revisions of imbalance prices. One auction in the five-zone market appears in the data once per zone, so 63 prices can show as 315 changes.

How the page handles it

A Total that moves when a further auction clears is marked as completed by a later auction, using the provider’s own auction rows. Completions are shown apart from revisions, and one auction across the joint market’s zones counts once. Where the stored provider rows cannot show which auctions changed, a change still counts as a revision.

9 November 2026: one revision can appear on several sources

What was found

The same revised price is often published by more than one source: 3 revised Finnish prices were in the log as 9 records.

How the page handles it

The headline counts each revised price once where several sources publish the same revision, and says how many changes were logged across how many sources.

28 October 2026: first findings about what the sources publish

From the first days of collection, 24 to 28 October 2026.

1. Most changes to a published row do not move a price

Sources often change other fields of a row they have published, such as satisfied demand, a direction or a capacity volume.

A change counts as a revision only if a euro price differs from its previous value.

2. A missing value is not always a withdrawal

No source withdrew a published value in this period. Two things looked like withdrawals:

  • On 26 October 2026 at 10:30 UTC, ENTSO-E answered one request without any NO2 rows. The 189 values came back at the next check.
  • eSett is queried in 31-day pieces. A query that started a few seconds past the quarter hour lost the interval on the boundary between two pieces: 12 values a day, one per zone, which came back the next day, 48 in all. nordic-balancing 1.0.0rc2 puts the boundaries on the quarter hour.

These entries are marked. A value counts as withdrawn only when two successful checks in a row miss it, and a marked entry must have its interval come back, so a real withdrawal stays visible.

3. eSett’s Danish placeholder is told apart by its spot difference

Shortly after each interval, eSett publishes a provisional row for DK1 and DK2 with every price at 0.00 before the real price. The row can carry an up or down direction, so the direction does not identify it. Its non-zero spot difference (imblSpotDifferencePrice) does. A real settled price of 0.00 has a spot difference of 0: there were three (DK1, 25 October 2026, 01:15 to 01:45 UTC, when the spot price was also 0.00).

nordic-balancing 1.0.0rc3 recognises the placeholder this way, checked against every Danish row from 2025 to October 2026 and against Energi Data Service. The collector has used it since 28 October 2026, 16:17 UTC. The 132 placeholder rows collected before that, and 2 rows re-read at the switch, are marked.

4. Energi Data Service recalculates its krone price once a day

Energi Data Service recalculates its Danish-krone imbalance price from the euro price once a day, around 16:30 UTC, moving it by a few øre while the euro price stays the same: 268 times in this period (125 DK1, 143 DK2). No euro imbalance price from Energi Data Service was revised. These are reported separately as currency recalculations, not as revisions.

5. eSett first publishes Finnish prices as 0.00

For about four in ten Finnish intervals, eSett first publishes the imbalance price as 0.00, about an hour after the interval ends, and replaces it with the settled price one to two hours later. Fingrid and ENTSO-E have the settled price from the start. Sweden, Norway and Denmark show no such rows. Up to 28 October 2026, 19:11 UTC, 176 of the 178 Finnish eSett changes were these replacements.

These 0.00 rows are marked as they are replaced, every 15 minutes, and count neither as a first price nor as a revision. A 0.00 is marked only when eSett later replaced it and Fingrid never showed 0.00 for that interval, so a real price of 0.00 (76 in the collected data, matched by Fingrid and ENTSO-E) stays.

The other two Finnish changes are revisions seen on more than one source: 25 October 2026, 00:00 UTC, from −15.77 to −2.35 (first in Fingrid, then ENTSO-E, then eSett), and 16 October 2026, 02:45 UTC, from −79.60 to −1.00 (eSett and ENTSO-E).

How the collector handles these

  • A value counts as withdrawn only when two successful checks in a row miss it.
  • A zone missing from an otherwise successful answer is never read as withdrawals.
  • Every check records the version of nordic-balancing that read it.
  • For DK1, DK2 and Finnish eSett rows, every distinct row the source sent is kept, so a reading can be checked against the original.
  • Automated tests cover each case above.
